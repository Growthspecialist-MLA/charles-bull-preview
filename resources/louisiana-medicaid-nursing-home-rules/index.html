Louisiana Medicaid Nursing Home Rules: 2026 Figures, Explained

The numbers Louisiana uses to decide who qualifies for Medicaid nursing home care, what a spouse can keep, and what happens to the home. Every figure links to its official source.

Louisiana's long-term care Medicaid rules decide whether Medicaid will help pay for a nursing home, and how much of a family's savings and income can be protected. Most of the dollar figures change every January, so an old chart can be badly out of date. These are the figures in effect for 2026.

Resources: what counts and what doesn't

As of 2026, a single person applying for long-term care Medicaid can have no more than $2,000 in countable resources. The limit is $3,000 only when both spouses need care. (LDH Z-700, opens in a new tab)

  • The home is generally not counted for eligibility, but equity above the yearly limit can block payment for nursing home care unless a spouse, a child under 21, or a blind or disabled child lives there. A home placed in a trust loses this protection. (LDH I-1630, opens in a new tab)
  • One vehicle per household is excluded, whatever its value, if anyone in the household uses it for transportation.
  • Life insurance with a total face value of $10,000 or less doesn't count. Above that, the cash surrender value of all policies counts. Term policies with no cash value don't count.

When one spouse needs care

Louisiana adds up the couple's combined countable resources, no matter whose name they are in. As of 2026, the at-home spouse can keep up to $162,660, and the spouse in care must be at or below $2,000. (LDH I-1660, opens in a new tab)

Income works differently. If the at-home spouse's own income is below Louisiana's spouse maintenance standard ($4,066.50 a month as of 2026), part of the nursing home spouse's income can be directed to them to make up the difference. (LDH I-1530, opens in a new tab)

A common mistake

Many charts list a couple's limit as $3,000. That only applies when both spouses are in care. When one spouse stays home, the protected amount is far higher.

Income

As of 2026, the income limit for an individual applicant is $2,982 a month, three times the federal SSI rate. Louisiana's rules include spend-down paths for some people above that amount. (CMS, opens in a new tab) Once on Medicaid, a nursing home resident keeps a $45 monthly personal needs allowance, and most of the rest of their income generally goes toward the cost of care.

The five-year look-back

Louisiana reviews transfers made in the 60 months before an application. A gift, or a sale for less than fair market value, can create a penalty period: a stretch of time when Medicaid will not pay for nursing home or waiver services. The length is figured by dividing the value transferred by $7,200 (the average monthly private-pay rate used for applications on or after September 1, 2024). The penalty is temporary, not a permanent disqualification, and exceptions and hardship rules exist. (LDH I-1670, opens in a new tab)

Estate recovery

After a Medicaid recipient dies, the Louisiana Department of Health must seek repayment from the estate for long-term care and related services received after age 55. Recovery is deferred while a spouse is living. Estates are exempt if the person left a child under 21 or a blind or disabled child. The state cannot recover against the first $15,000 or one-half of the median homestead value in the parish, whichever is higher, and hardship waivers are available. (La. R.S. 46:153.4, opens in a new tab)

Care at home instead of a facility

Louisiana's Community Choices Waiver provides home and community services to adults 21 and older who qualify for Medicaid and need a nursing-home level of care, including Monitored In-Home Caregiving with a live-in caregiver. The waiver has a registry, or waiting list. (LDH CCW fact sheet, opens in a new tab)

What to do with this

These rules interact, and the right move depends on your family, your property and your timing. A mistake, like giving the house to a child right before applying, can cost months of coverage. Talk it through before you act. See Medicaid and nursing home planning.
