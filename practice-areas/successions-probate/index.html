Successions & Probate · Welsh, Louisiana

Successions & Probate Attorney in Welsh, Louisiana

When someone you love dies, Louisiana law requires a succession to settle their affairs and transfer their property. Charles handles the legal work so your family can grieve.

Louisiana handles inheritance differently from every other state. The process of settling an estate is called a succession, and it can be complicated and slow if it is handled poorly. Charles Bull helps families open a succession, settle the estate and transfer property without costly mistakes or unnecessary disputes.

Two kinds of succession

  • Testate succession: the person left a valid will (in Louisiana, a testament). The succession carries out its instructions.
  • Intestate succession: the person died without a valid will. Louisiana law decides who inherits.

In both cases, the succession establishes who the surviving spouse and heirs are, who the legatees are, and what property and debts the estate holds.

Why families open a succession

  • To transfer a house, land or other real estate into the heirs' names
  • To pay the debts of the person who died, and limit the heirs' exposure to them
  • To resolve legal claims the person had
  • To manage property inherited by minors or stepchildren
  • To manage or transfer a business the person owned

Small successions

Not every estate needs a full court proceeding. As of 2026, Louisiana treats the estate of a Louisiana resident worth $200,000 or less as a small succession, up from $125,000 before a 2026 change in the law. (La. C.C.P. art. 3421, opens in a new tab) Many small successions can be handled with a sworn affidavit signed by the heirs instead of opening a court case. The affidavit can be used with banks and recorded in the parish conveyance records to transfer real estate.

Administration: full or independent

When an estate needs to be administered, the court appoints an executor or administrator to collect and inventory property, deal with creditors and pay debts. In a full administration, many of those steps need court approval.

A faster path when everyone agrees

Louisiana also allows independent administration, which lets the estate representative act without asking the court to approve each step. It is available when the will provides for it, or when the main legatees (or, with no will, all of the heirs) agree. It can cut both expense and delay.

What is a succession?

A succession is Louisiana's legal process for settling a person's estate after death. It identifies the heirs or legatees, pays the debts, and transfers property, such as a house or land, into the new owners' names. Other states call this probate.

Do we need a succession if there was no will?

Often, yes. When someone dies without a valid will, it is called an intestate succession, and Louisiana law decides who inherits. A succession may still be needed to transfer real estate or other property to those heirs.

What is independent administration?

Independent administration lets the succession representative handle the estate without asking the court to approve each action. It is available when the will provides for it, or when all of the main legatees, or with no will all of the heirs, agree. It can reduce time and expense.

How long does a succession take?

It depends on the size of the estate, whether there is a valid will, and whether the heirs agree. Simple successions with cooperative families move much faster than contested ones. Charles can give you a realistic picture once he reviews your situation.
