Medicaid & Nursing Homes · Welsh, Louisiana

Medicaid & Nursing Home Planning Attorney in Welsh, Louisiana

Nursing home care can drain a lifetime of savings in a few years. Charles helps families plan for that cost, qualify for Medicaid, and keep what Louisiana law allows.

Families move a parent into a nursing home for many reasons. Whatever the reason, figuring out how to pay is complicated. Without a plan, savings, retirement funds and sometimes the family home can go toward the cost of care.

Charles Bull served on the board of the Welsh nursing home for almost ten years. He helps families understand how Louisiana Medicaid works, plan ahead where they can, and apply correctly when care is needed now.

How Medicaid fits in

Medicaid is a public health program for people with limited income and resources, and many families rely on it to pay for nursing home care. Each state runs its own program, and Louisiana has its own rules. To qualify for long-term care, a person generally needs to meet a nursing-home level of care, an income limit and a resource limit.

Protecting the spouse at home

When one spouse needs nursing home care and the other stays home, Louisiana looks at the couple's combined resources, not just what is in one name. The at-home spouse can keep a protected share, and part of the nursing home spouse's income can go to the at-home spouse if their own income is low. (LDH I-1660, opens in a new tab)

Common misunderstanding

A married couple does not have to spend down to a few thousand dollars when only one spouse needs care. The lower limit applies to the spouse in care. Getting this wrong leads families to spend money they could have kept.

The five-year look-back

Louisiana reviews transfers made in the 60 months before a Medicaid application. Giving property away or selling it for less than it is worth during that window can create a penalty period, during which Medicaid will not pay for nursing home or waiver services. (LDH I-1670, opens in a new tab) Exceptions exist, so talk with an attorney before you move or give away assets.

Care at home: the Community Choices Waiver

Louisiana's Community Choices Waiver serves adults 21 and older who qualify for Medicaid and need a nursing-home level of care, with services at home instead of in a facility. One of those services is Monitored In-Home Caregiving, where a caregiver lives with the participant and receives a daily stipend, supported by a care manager and a nurse. (LDH MIHC fact sheet, opens in a new tab) The waiver has a waiting list, so getting on it early matters. Charles helps families through that process.

After death: estate recovery

After a Medicaid recipient dies, the Louisiana Department of Health must seek repayment from the estate for long-term care received after age 55. Recovery is deferred while a spouse is living, and estates are exempt if the person left a child under 21 or a blind or disabled child. State law also protects a minimum amount and allows hardship waivers. (La. R.S. 46:153.4, opens in a new tab) Planning can affect what your family eventually inherits.

FAQ

Common questions

Don't see your question? Call (337) 329-9243.

Will we lose the house if Mom or Dad goes on Medicaid?

Not necessarily. Louisiana generally does not count the home when deciding eligibility, though very high home equity can be a barrier unless a spouse or certain children live there. A home placed in a trust loses that protection. After death, the state may make a claim against the estate, with exemptions for a surviving spouse and certain children.

What is the five-year look-back?

When someone applies for Medicaid nursing home or waiver services, Louisiana reviews transfers made in the 60 months before applying. A gift or a sale for less than fair market value in that window can trigger a penalty period during which Medicaid will not pay for that care.

My spouse needs a nursing home. Do I have to spend everything?

No. When one spouse enters care and the other stays home, Louisiana lets the at-home spouse keep a substantial amount of the couple's resources, and can direct part of the nursing home spouse's income to the at-home spouse. The exact figures change each January.

Does Medicaid pay for care at home?

Louisiana's Community Choices Waiver provides services at home and in the community for adults 21 and older who qualify for Medicaid and need a nursing-home level of care. There is a registry, or waiting list, for the waiver.

Is it too late to plan if care is needed now?

Often not. Families facing a nursing home admission today still have options under the rules for spouses, exempt property and income. Planning early gives you more choices, but a crisis is still a good time to get advice.
